Interest rates heavily influence bonds, directly affecting their prices, yields and overall investment attractiveness. Understanding how they respond to interest rate changes is central to fixed-income analysis. However, like other financial instruments, bonds do not operate in isolation, movements in one bond can trigger shifts across other. This paper will aim to investigate how interest rates impact bonds, through an additional lens: the interactions between bonds themselves. To examine the impact, an agent-based modelling approach will be undertaken, as it allows to stimulate those micro-level interactions and observe collective market-level outcomes. Such a perspective offers a nuanced evaluation of how bonds react to changes in the interest rate environment.

## Dataset
The dataset ‘IBOXX Fixed Income Index’ is used for the purpose of this investigation. The pre-cleaned dataset contains 1482 entries and 129 features, spanning bond identification, yield metrics, pricing, spreads, and other risk metrics. 60 of the features were float, 38 integers and 31 as objects. The data cleaning process included removing empty columns (10 columns with more than 70% null rates), outlier rows (row 79 and 829), dropping redundant features, such as keeping only ISIN for bond identification. The number of features was reduced by 47, with 44 float, 24 integer and 14 object columns. The preprocessing reduced data dimensionality significantly retaining key variables affecting bond pricing and risk. Moreover, time-related features were processed, like converting Next Coupon Date into a numerical format, and log transformation was applied to the Z-spread, preparing the dataset for modelling.

## ODD Protocol
To generally explain the methodology behind the model an overview, design concepts and details (ODD) protocol will be used, as introduced by Grimm et al. (2006). This is a standardized framework that allows to report ABMs in a way that is clear, structured and replicable. Only overview and design concepts, however, will be covered as implementation details are irrelevant to the research question. 

### Overview 
The purpose of the model is to investigate how bond core features, prices, spread and liquidity change in response to interest rate shocks. The research further explores how the effect propagates within peers’ groups i.e. bonds with the same ratings. Such simulation provides insights into how market dynamics evolve over time under stress conditions like a change in interest rates. 

The entities have defining state variables. The bond agent is identified by its ISIN and has the following attributes: Issuer Country, Level 5 classification, Duration, Convexity, Yield to Maturity, Index Price, Z-spread (log), Bid-Ask Spread, iBoxx Rating, Coupon rate, Time to Maturity. The attributes come from the dataset, and using ISIN, other attributes can be mapped. Due to the scope of this research, those features are not included, as they will not change during the simulation. The temporal scale of the model is defined in discrete time steps, with each time step corresponding to one second of market time. Spatial scale is not explicit in the model, as bonds are not grouped by geography. 

The model’s process and scheduling involve initialization, interest rate shock, propagation and updating. Using the dataset the model is populated with bond agents and each bond is assigned price, z-spread, and bid-ask spread alongside its history that tracks changes over time. When an interest rate shock occurs, the model updates each bond’s price, credit spread, and liquidity measures. The magnitude of the response is dependent on the size of the shock as well as the coefficients derived from an OLS regression which will be discussed in detail in section 3. After the initial shock, the average change in price, spread, and liquidity within peer groups is calculated and further propagated back to every agent in that group. At each time step, the value of bond’s attributes is saved to analyse the dynamic history of the bond agents. 

### Design concept

The model’s basic principles are covered by two main response mechanisms:

1.	Interest rate sensitivity: bond attributes respond to interest rate shocks, as reflected by regression-based coefficients
2.	Peer-group contagion: a change in bond’s attributes leads change in peers 

Market-level behaviours emerge as a consequence of individual bonds adapting to interest rate environment and feedback effects from peer groups. Moreover, the agents can sense interest rate shocks and peer-group changes. The interaction between bonds in the model occur indirectly through peer-group contagion, rather than direct interactions. When an interest rate shock is stimulated the average peer change is further propagated amongst the group. The magnitude of how bond’s behaviour influences its peers is specified by constant contagion coefficient, representing sensitivity of a bond to peer-driven effects. Peer groups are defined by rating and are grouped into units as collectives. A random element is incorporated in the simulation. Considering the ABM is modelling the financial market, which inherently exhibits stochastic behaviour, the noise term introduced in the simulation is drawn from a normal distribution. Although normal distribution does not capture fully the nuances of financial markets, it is used for theoretical and practical simplicity. The model output is collected through historical logging of price, spread and liquidity. No bond agent objectives are explicitly stated, and they do not learn over time. Finally, the model stimulates only one interest rate shock, and the consequent changes are driven by bond-to-bond interactions and the random noise.
